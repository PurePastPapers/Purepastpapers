## AQA_SOC_4.3.1_b1 | content

Q: What did Durkheim call a condition where shared norms lose their force?
A: Anomie
Q: What did Durkheim say crime can do for social change?
A: Make social boundaries open to change
Q: What does Merton mean by anomie in strain theory?
A: A gap between cultural goals and legitimate means
Q: What is innovation in Merton’s modes of adaptation?
A: Accepting success goals but using illegitimate means
Q: What is ritualism in Merton’s modes of adaptation?
A: Abandoning success goals while following approved means
Q: What is retreatism in Merton’s modes of adaptation?
A: Rejecting both success goals and approved means
Q: What is rebellion in Merton’s modes of adaptation?
A: Replacing existing goals and means with alternatives
Q: Which sociologist argued crime can be a response to status frustration?
A: Albert Cohen
Q: Which sociologists linked delinquent subcultures to unequal access to illegitimate opportunity?
A: Cloward and Ohlin
Q: Which sociologist argued working-class culture values toughness and trouble?
A: Walter Miller
Q: What does differential association theory say people learn through close interaction?
A: Definitions favourable to lawbreaking
Q: Which criminologist developed differential association theory?
A: Edwin Sutherland
Q: What did Matza call the temporary movement between conventional and delinquent values?
A: Drift
Q: What is a technique of neutralisation?
A: An account that excuses or minimises deviant conduct
Q: Which interactionist argued that social reactions can create deviant identities?
A: Howard Becker
Q: What is primary deviance in Lemert’s theory?
A: Rule-breaking that does not reshape a person’s identity
Q: What is secondary deviance in Lemert’s theory?
A: Rule-breaking shaped by a deviant label and identity
Q: What does Hirschi’s control theory say reduces offending?
A: Strong social bonds
Q: Which social bond did Hirschi identify as the fear of losing others’ approval?
A: Attachment
Q: What does Marxist criminology argue about law-making?
A: Marxists argue that law reflects powerful groups’ interests
Q: What is the left realist response to the claim that crime is only a ruling-class label?
A: Working-class people also suffer real victimisation
