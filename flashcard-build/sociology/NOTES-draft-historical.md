## Sources and date-sensitive material

The official AQA subject-content pages opened for Education/Methods and Crime are used to check the route and leaf mapping. ONS and government sources were opened for current figures. Named studies are linked in `sources.tsv` to publisher, journal or public research sources. The source URL is a lead where the row says `no`; that verdict must not be treated as confirmation.

Statistics are tied to their reference period in the front: education attainment 2023/24, UK household and family statistics 2025, England and Wales births/fertility 2025, UK life tables 2022–24, Census religion 2021, and crime year ending March 2024 or 2025. These figures should be refreshed before a later release is used as current evidence.

## Limits to address in independent review

- Ethnicity has fewer education attainment examples than class and gender. The cards include school attainment by disadvantage and sex, plus named explanations, but the ethnic pattern needs careful checking against the selected comparison and year.
- The crime class distribution is difficult to represent with official data because recorded data do not classify offending by social class. The set includes theories and a methods limitation; do not invent a class-by-crime statistic. The belief/religion by class, gender and ethnicity leaf has theory and named sociologists but lacks a comparative group statistic.
- The Methods in Context cards give school-specific access, consent, behaviour, validity and method points, but the reviewer should check each against the particular method and avoid treating a generic methods claim as guaranteed.
- The set includes relevant background cards but the reviewer should prune any background fact that does not materially help explain a sociological topic. ONS migration estimates are provisional; the front fixes the reference period and November 2026 publication date.
- 449 atomic cards remain independently unverified. The reviewer must either confirm each against the cited page/source or cut it. No random 10% error rate is claimed at this author stage.
