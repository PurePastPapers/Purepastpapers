Q: Assess the claim that globalisation shows religion is becoming more significant. State one supporting argument, one qualification and a judgement.
A: Global communication and migration can spread religions and enable transnational communities, supporting the claim that religion remains influential. ## AQA_SOC_MARK | mark_scheme
Q: An item says that more people live alone or cohabit than in the past, while the married nuclear family remains common. Using the item and your knowledge, evaluate sociological explanations of family diversity. (20-mark Paper 2 format)
A: The item suggests both greater diversity and continued importance of the married family. Postmodern and personal-life approaches explain varied pathways through greater choice and negotiated relationships; lone-parent, reconstituted and living-apart-together arrangements illustrate different structures. However, choices are shaped by resources, gender relations and policy, so diversity should not be reduced to free individual choice. Chester’s neo-conventional argument also cautions that the nuclear family remains significant, while that does not make it the only form or show that it best serves everyone. Overall, family life is diverse in structure and pathway, but its extent depends on the measure and social group considered.<p><strong>Marking points</strong></p><ul><li>Apply both sides of the item: diversity and continued prevalence of marriage</li><li>Explain relevant perspectives and examples of varied family forms</li><li>Develop evaluation of individual choice and structural constraints</li><li>Reach a qualified conclusion about extent and measurement</li></ul><p><strong>Common trap</strong></p>Avoid treating the presence of more forms as proof that the nuclear family has disappeared, or treating prevalence as proof it is universal.
