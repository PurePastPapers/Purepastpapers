Q: How does Williams use lighting to present illusion in A Streetcar Named Desire?
A: Model answer: Blanche avoids bright light, suggesting her fear of exposure. Marking points: Analyse a stage technique and link it to illusion and context. Common trap: Retelling Blanche's past without analysing the lighting.
Q: Compare how Frankenstein and The Handmaid's Tale use narrative structure to shape knowledge.
A: Model answer: Shelley's embedded narratives offer competing perspectives, while Atwood's night sections interrupt Gilead's daily routine. Marking points: Compare narrative methods, support the argument with both texts, and relate relevant context. Common trap: Discussing each novel separately with no connection.
Q: How does Iago create suspicion in Othello?
A: Model answer: Iago echoes Othello's words and withholds apparent knowledge, making Othello supply the fear. Marking points: Analyse dramatic language, develop an argument and engage with relevant context and critical interpretations. Common trap: Retelling the plot without examining dramatic method.
Q: Compare how two poems present place, using Blake's London and another Romantic poem.
A: Model answer: Blake's moving speaker presents an oppressive city, while Wordsworth surveys the landscape in Tintern Abbey. Marking points: Analyse both poets' methods, connect the poems and integrate relevant context. Common trap: Writing a historical context paragraph without linking it to the poems.
