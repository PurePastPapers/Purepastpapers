# Independent GCSE History review

This is a bounded source review of the cards currently in `draft/all-cards.json`, generated on 25 November 2026. It is not an approval or a ready-to-upload sign-off.

## Verdict counts

- Retained cards checked for a verdict: 116 (80 exam, 31 background, 5 technique).
- `verified=yes`: 31.
- `verified=no`: 85. These rows have a named source lead in `sources.tsv`, but the specific wording or claim was not confirmed from the opened text. They must not be treated as verified. I did not invent a yes verdict to fill the ledger.
- Cards cut or materially corrected during review: 26 from the initial draft. The retained set now compiles with 23/23 leaves represented, but most leaves remain thin.
- Verulamium: all 8 retained cards flagged for Will's required source review. Each has named St Albans Museums/Historic England references; the museum overview and Historic England material were opened. Six related facts (theatre/walls, 1930 Wheelers, Boudicca and Roman site context) align with the opened material, but the ledger remains deliberately conservative where the exact detail was not confirmed.

## Corrections and cuts

- Removed the claim that hot-iron ordeal was a medieval punishment within this route; OCR's c.1250–1500 crime content does not include it and the practice is normally dated earlier.
- Removed the overgeneralised claim that hanging was the usual first-offence theft punishment (identified by the parent reviewer).
- Reworded frankpledge to collective tithing responsibility, rather than claiming that the tithing specifically reported crimes.
- Removed the 1717 Transportation Act card because the date/title is inconsistently reported and no opened primary source established the wording. Removed the duplicate 1601 Poor Law card (parent reviewer).
- Changed Human Rights Act wording to domestic effect for Convention rights.
- Removed the misleading claim that William's suppression of the 1069 northern rebellion was itself called the Harrying; retained the campaign card and removed a duplicate. Removed the unsupported 1075 revolt card.
- Changed bailey definition to “beside the motte”; removed a duplicate hypocaust card and replaced an unsupported “principal evidence” claim with the Wheeler excavation card.
- Corrected the Great Heathen Army question from 865 to 866. Removed duplicate Alfred/Edington, Harold/Hardrada and Gestapo cards.
- Replaced the simplistic “Treaty of Wedmore” card with a narrower 886 Alfred–Guthrum boundary claim.
- Removed generic background filler on raid/invasion, settlement, kingdom, treaty, censorship, etc. where it added vocabulary rather than historical context. Removed the non-canonical “Volga Vikings” label.
- Corrected the J411/33 argument question from 12 to 18 marks using OCR's public June 2024 mark scheme. Removed a “further-research question” technique card because no matching J411 prompt was confirmed.

## Remaining limitations and gaps

- `sources.tsv` contains a verdict for every retained stable key; 85 no verdicts identify claims that still need direct source verification or removal. Areas with no-verdicts include medieval crime practice, general Norman and Anglo-Saxon context, some Viking geography/trade details, most Nazi social-policy and occupied-Europe facts, and generic historical-method definitions.
- The set is substantively incomplete, despite technical leaf coverage. Crime has few examples across the long c.1250–present span; Norman Conquest has little on succession claims, military events, rebellions, governance and landholding; Vikings has little on the Scandinavian homelands, eastward expansion and settlement/cultural change; Nazi Germany has little on police terror, youth/women, persecution, wartime society and the Holocaust; Verulamium lacks site-plan/feature analysis, development over time and wider regional context.
- Most leaves have only 3–5 exam cards and 1–2 background cards. Several backgrounds remain low-value and need better historical context.
- The J411/33 interpretation-comparison technique card remains `no`: OCR's public paper/mark scheme supports the usefulness question and the 18-mark arguments, but the comparison wording was not confirmed.
- There are no worked historical source-analysis examples; the site-study skill deck is also absent. The current five technique cards do not provide a full exam-method deck.
- OCR spec verification supports route coverage and thematic scope, not every historical detail. The named URLs are source leads, not proof for any row with `verified=no`.
