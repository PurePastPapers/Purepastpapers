## Norman Conquest

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_NORMAN_2.1: Anglo-Saxon religion

Q: Which Anglo-Saxon monastery became a mission base for converting the northern kingdoms?
A: Lindisfarne

Source: English Heritage, “Significance of Lindisfarne Priory,” https://www.english-heritage.org.uk/visit/places/lindisfarne-priory/history/significance/ — lines 227–234 state that the monastery founded in 635 became the base for converting the northern Anglo-Saxon kingdoms from paganism to Christianity.

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_NORMAN_2.2: pre-Conquest Norman feudal practice

Q: What feudal service did vavassors owe Mont-Saint-Michel after it acquired properties around 1024?
A: Military service

Source: David Crouch, “Military Service in Normandy Before 1066,” Anglo-Norman Studies V, Cambridge University Press, https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/anglonorman-studies-v/military-service-in-normandy-before-1066/2DDF6F5AC5563F8239D4CCE4E1D0A58C — lines 466 onward discuss the military service owed to the abbey by vavassors after it acquired properties around 1024. Caution: the same passage says the claim that the duke had imposed all the service earlier rests on a later 1157 case, so this card tests the documented obligation only, not that disputed explanation.

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_NORMAN_2.5: Domesday compilation

Q: How did Domesday commissioners verify evidence in each district?
A: By taking evidence on oath and using a local jury

Source: Herefordshire Through Time, “How was the Domesday Book created?”, https://htt.herefordshire.gov.uk/herefordshires-past/the-medieval-period/the-domesday-survey/how-was-the-domesday-book-created/ — lines 41–50 describe commissioners taking sworn evidence, using local juries to verify facts, and the information being collated at Winchester.

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_NORMAN_2.5: named legal continuity

Q: Which earlier king’s law did William’s recorded laws say people should continue to hold?
A: King Edward’s law

Source: Fordham University, Medieval Sourcebook, “Laws of William the Conqueror,” https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/source/will1-lawsb.asp — lines 22–30 include the recorded decree that people should “have and hold” the law of King Edward. The same clause allows additions, so this is continuity claimed in the text, not a claim that Norman law remained unchanged.

## Viking Expansion

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_VIKING_4.4: Greenland settlement pressures

Q: What climate change stressed Norse farming in Greenland from the mid-thirteenth century?
A: Longer, harsher winters

Source: “Disequilibrium, Adaptation, and the Norse Settlement of Greenland,” https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6182579/ — lines 192–198 describe mid-thirteenth- and fourteenth-century cooling, longer and harsher winters, reduced productivity and greater livestock mortality. This wording identifies a pressure, not a single proven cause of abandonment.

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_VIKING_4.5: Svein’s campaign

Q: Which town did Swein Forkbeard’s army storm before advancing into Wiltshire in 1003?
A: Exeter

Source: The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, annal 1003, https://www.melocki.org.uk/anglosaxon/Chronicle.html — lines 2260–2272 say Exeter was entered by storm, the army then advanced into Wiltshire, and Swein subsequently led the force to Milton.

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_VIKING_4.5: Cnut’s administration

Q: Which earldom did Cnut assign to Thorkell in the 1017 division of England?
A: East Anglia

Source: Ryan Lavelle, “Cnut, King of the English, 1017–19,” University of Winchester, https://cris.winchester.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/20094571/11780050_Lavelle_CnutKingEnglish_withstatement.pdf — lines 7–10 identify East Anglia as Thorkell’s division in 1017.

## Nazi Germany

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_NAZI_5.2: political courts

Q: Which court did Hitler create in Berlin in 1934 for treason and major political cases?
A: The People’s Court

Source: United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, “Law and Justice in the Third Reich,” https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/law-and-justice-in-the-third-reich — lines 135–136 state that Hitler established special courts in 1933 and ordered the People’s Court created in Berlin in 1934 to try treason and important political cases.

### OCR_GCSE_HIST_NAZI_5.2: propaganda effectiveness

Q: Which party did many German workers support despite Nazi efforts to win them over?
A: The Social Democratic Party

Source: United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, “Selling Nazism in a Democracy,” https://www.ushmm.org/information/exhibitions/online-exhibitions/propaganda/selling-nazism-in-a-democracy — lines 220–221 say Nazi propaganda aimed at workers had limited success and much of the working class supported the Social Democratic Party or Communist Party. This card selects one named example while preserving the source’s “many,” not all, qualification.
