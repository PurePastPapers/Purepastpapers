## AQA_GEOG_3.2.2.1 | content
Q: In human geography, what is the difference between space and place?
A: Space has no meaning attached, while place is space people have given meaning to
Q: Which geographer wrote that "what begins as undifferentiated space becomes place as we get to know it better and endow it with value"?
A: Yi-Fu Tuan
Q: In which 1977 book did Yi-Fu Tuan set out his distinction between space and place?
A: Space and Place: The Perspective of Experience
Q: What term did Yi-Fu Tuan use for the emotional bond or love between people and a place?
A: Topophilia
Q: Which geographer popularised the term topophilia in a 1974 book of the same name?
A: Yi-Fu Tuan
Q: What is the term for a fear or strong dislike of a place?
A: Topophobia
Q: Which humanistic geographer wrote Place and Placelessness (1976)?
A: Edward Relph
Q: What did Edward Relph mean by placelessness?
A: Loss of distinctive local identity, so places look alike and feel alike
Q: According to Relph, which kind of culture spreads placelessness through chain stores, mass tourism and standard buildings?
A: Mass culture
Q: In Relph's terms, what is existential insideness?
A: The deepest belonging, where a person feels completely at home in a place without thinking about it
Q: In Relph's terms, what is existential outsideness?
A: A deep sense of alienation, feeling a place is strange and you do not belong there
Q: In Relph's terms, what is objective outsideness?
A: Viewing a place in a detached, scientific way, e.g. as a planner or researcher
Q: In Relph's terms, what is vicarious insideness?
A: Feeling attached to a place experienced second-hand, e.g. through a novel, film or painting
Q: Which geographer argued in 1987 that place has three aspects: location, locale and sense of place?
A: John Agnew
Q: In Agnew's three aspects of place, what is location?
A: Where the place is, its fixed position on the Earth's surface (e.g. coordinates)
Q: In Agnew's three aspects of place, what is locale?
A: The physical setting for everyday life, e.g. buildings, streets and parks
Q: In Agnew's three aspects of place, what is sense of place?
A: The feelings and emotional attachment people have to a place
Q: Which geographer wrote Place: A Short Introduction (2004) and defined place as a "meaningful location"?
A: Tim Cresswell
Q: Which anthropologist coined the term non-places in a 1992 book?
A: Marc Augé
Q: What did Marc Augé mean by non-places?
A: Spaces of transit people pass through without forming attachment, e.g. airports, motorways
Q: Which term did Augé link to the growth of non-places, describing an excess of time, space and individualism?
A: Supermodernity
Q: Which geographer argued that places are open and shaped by flows, not bounded and fixed, in 'A Global Sense of Place' (1991)?
A: Doreen Massey
Q: What is meant by Massey's progressive (global) sense of place?
A: A place's identity comes from its links to other places, not from an inward-looking past
Q: According to Massey, does a place have one single, fixed identity?
A: No: places have multiple identities that keep changing
Q: What term did Massey use for the way different groups have unequal control over flows and movement between places?
A: Power geometry
Q: What is meant by sense of place?
A: The subjective feelings and attachments a person has towards a place
Q: What is meant by place identity?
A: The distinctive character of a place as people recognise it, e.g. a 'mining town'
Q: What is an insider perspective on place?
A: The view of someone who feels at home in and belongs to a place
Q: What is an outsider perspective on place?
A: The view of someone who feels a stranger in a place, e.g. a newcomer or tourist
Q: Why does a long-term resident usually see a place differently from a tourist?
A: Daily lived experience gives them deeper knowledge and attachment (insider perspective)
Q: Why are insider and outsider not fixed categories for a person?
A: People move between them, e.g. a migrant becomes an insider over time
Q: What is meant by a near place?
A: A place a person feels close to and familiar with, physically or emotionally
Q: What is meant by a far place?
A: A place a person feels distant from and unfamiliar with, with little emotional attachment
Q: How does a place on the other side of the world become a near place for someone?
A: Emotional ties, e.g. family links or regular visits, make it feel close
Q: How does a place physically close to home become a far place for someone?
A: People feel no connection to it, e.g. an area they avoid or never visit
Q: What is an experienced place?
A: A place you have lived in or visited, so you know it first-hand
Q: What is a media place?
A: A place known only through media (e.g. TV, film, books, songs, art)
Q: Why is a media place able to become an experienced place, but not the reverse?
A: Visiting it makes it experienced, but a visit cannot be undone
Q: Why do visitors to a media place often feel disappointed?
A: The media image was selective, so reality does not match expectations
Q: Why do media places often give an extreme image of a place?
A: Media focus on very positive or very negative features, e.g. crime stories
Q: Why do fictional places, such as Hogwarts, still create a sense of place?
A: Media representations give people strong feelings about them without a visit
Q: In the context of place, what are endogenous factors?
A: Internal factors that originate within a place and shape its character
Q: In the context of place, what are exogenous factors?
A: External factors: a place's relationships and connections with other places
Q: What is the site of a place?
A: The physical land it is built on (e.g. relief, drainage, soils, water supply)
Q: What is the situation of a place?
A: Its position relative to other places, routeways and surrounding features
Q: Which type of factor, endogenous or exogenous, is a place's situation and accessibility to other places?
A: Exogenous (relationship with other places)
Q: What does topography mean?
A: The shape and form of the land surface and its features
Q: How does valley topography affect the character of a village built along it?
A: It forms a linear village and a feeling of enclosure and isolation
Q: How does local rock type shape the character of a place's built environment?
A: Buildings are made from local stone, giving a distinctive look
Q: How does coastal location shape the character of a place?
A: It supports functions such as ports, fishing or seaside tourism
Q: How does land use shape the character of a place?
A: It decides whether a place feels rural, urban, residential or industrial
Q: How does the built environment shape the character of a place?
A: Its architecture, age and density set the visual character, e.g. Georgian terraces
Q: How does infrastructure shape the character of a place?
A: Transport hubs such as ports, airports and railways make places busy and connected
Q: How does an ageing population shape the character of a place?
A: It drives demand for services such as care homes and healthcare
Q: How does ethnic diversity shape the character of a place?
A: It adds cultural features such as places of worship, food shops and festivals
Q: How does the main employment sector shape the character of a place?
A: It shapes land use, buildings and wealth, e.g. a primary-industry or finance town
Q: Which endogenous factor is shown by a place's age-sex structure?
A: Demographic characteristics
Q: Which endogenous factor is shown by a place's average income and employment types?
A: Economic characteristics
Q: What is a clone town?
A: A town whose high street is dominated by the same chain stores as other towns
Q: Why is a clone town an example of Relph's placelessness?
A: Chain stores make it look and feel like any other town, losing local identity
Q: Why is place important in human life and experience?
A: Places give people identity, belonging, security and memories
Q: What is place attachment?
A: The emotional bond a person forms with a particular place
Q: In which magazine was Massey's essay 'A Global Sense of Place' published in June 1991?
A: Marxism Today

## AQA_GEOG_3.2.2.2.1 | content
Q: How does in-migration change the demographic characteristics of a place?
A: It changes age structure and ethnic mix, usually adding young adults
Q: How does out-migration of young adults affect the age structure of a place?
A: The population ages and the birth rate falls
Q: How does international migration change the cultural characteristics of a place?
A: It adds new languages, religions, foods and festivals
Q: What is a remittance?
A: Money sent home by migrants to family in their place of origin
Q: What is foreign direct investment (FDI)?
A: Investment by a company in business operations in another country
Q: What is deindustrialisation?
A: The decline of manufacturing industry in a place or country
Q: What is the main cause of deindustrialisation in the UK since the 1970s?
A: Global shift of manufacturing to lower-cost countries (globalisation)
Q: What is disinvestment?
A: The withdrawal of investment from a place, e.g. factory closure
Q: What is the negative multiplier effect?
A: A downward spiral where job losses cut spending, so more businesses close
Q: Which economist described cumulative causation, where growth or decline in a place reinforces itself?
A: Gunnar Myrdal
Q: What is the positive multiplier effect?
A: New investment creates jobs and spending, which creates more jobs
Q: What is globalisation?
A: Growing interconnection of places through flows of trade, people, money and ideas
Q: Which geographer used the term time-space compression in The Condition of Postmodernity (1989)?
A: David Harvey
Q: What is time-space compression?
A: Faster transport and communications make places feel closer together
Q: Who coined the term gentrification in 1964?
A: Ruth Glass
Q: What is gentrification?
A: Wealthier people move into a poorer area, improving housing and displacing poorer residents
Q: What is one negative impact of gentrification on original residents?
A: Rising rents and house prices force them to move out (displacement)
Q: What is studentification?
A: Large numbers of students moving into an area, changing housing and services
Q: What is counter-urbanisation?
A: Movement of people from urban areas to rural areas
Q: What is rural depopulation?
A: Decline in a rural area's population, usually as young people move away
Q: What is economic change in the context of place?
A: A shift in a place's jobs and economy, e.g. from manufacturing to services
Q: What is the quaternary sector?
A: Knowledge-based jobs such as research, IT and finance
Q: Which model shows a country's employment moving from primary to secondary to tertiary as it develops?
A: Clark-Fisher model
Q: What is social inequality?
A: Unequal access to resources such as income, housing, health and education
Q: What is deprivation?
A: Lacking things society sees as necessary (e.g. income, jobs, decent housing)
Q: What is the cycle of deprivation?
A: Poverty causes poor health and education, which keeps the next generation poor
Q: What is an ethnic enclave?
A: An area where one ethnic group is concentrated, with its own shops and services
Q: What is an external force on a place?
A: An influence from outside, e.g. government policy, TNC decision or global institution
Q: How does a TNC decision to close a factory act as an exogenous force?
A: A decision made elsewhere removes jobs and investment from the place
Q: How does government policy act as an external force on a place?
A: It directs funding and planning, e.g. regeneration grants or enterprise zones
Q: What is an enterprise zone?
A: An area given tax breaks and simpler planning to attract businesses
Q: What is regeneration?
A: Long-term improvement of a declining area's economy, environment and society
Q: How do past connections shape a place today?
A: Old trade, industry or migration links leave buildings, communities and identity
Q: How do former colonial links shape migration to UK places?
A: They created migration flows from former colonies, forming diaspora communities
Q: How does a port embed a place in the global scale?
A: Its trade links connect it to places worldwide
Q: Which geographer described places as shaped by successive layered 'rounds of investment' in Spatial Divisions of Labour (1984)?
A: Doreen Massey
Q: What is a palimpsest in the study of place?
A: A place where layers from different periods are visible together
Q: What is a diaspora?
A: A population that has spread from its homeland but keeps links with it
Q: What is homogenisation of place?
A: Places becoming more alike due to global brands and culture

## AQA_GEOG_3.2.2.2.2 | content
Q: What is place meaning?
A: The ideas, feelings and associations people attach to a place
Q: Why do older and younger people often attach different meanings to one place?
A: Their lived experience of the place differs, e.g. older people remember its past
Q: What is lived experience of place?
A: A person's direct, everyday experience of living in a place
Q: What is positionality?
A: How a person's identity and background shape their view of a place
Q: Name one aspect of identity that shapes how a person perceives a place.
A: Age (also: gender, ethnicity, religion, sexuality, income)
Q: Why do women and men often perceive the same street differently at night?
A: Differences in feeling safe shape their experience of the place
Q: What is meant by a contested place meaning?
A: Different groups hold conflicting meanings for the same place
Q: What is place representation?
A: The way a place is presented to others, e.g. in art, media or statistics
Q: What is rebranding a place?
A: Changing a place's image to attract investment, visitors or residents
Q: What is the difference between rebranding and regeneration?
A: Rebranding changes a place's image, while regeneration changes its economy and environment
Q: What is place marketing?
A: Promoting a place to outsiders, e.g. with slogans, logos and campaigns
Q: What is placemaking?
A: Shaping public spaces, often with the community, to strengthen people's connection to them
Q: What is a flagship project in regeneration?
A: A large, eye-catching development meant to lead regeneration and change image
Q: What is culture-led regeneration?
A: Using arts, museums and events to improve a place's economy and image
Q: What is heritage-led regeneration?
A: Reusing historic buildings and a place's past to attract visitors and investment
Q: What is sports-led regeneration?
A: Using major sports events and venues to regenerate an area
Q: What is retail-led regeneration?
A: Using new shopping centres to bring investment and jobs to an area
Q: Which type of external agency includes national government and local councils?
A: Government agencies
Q: Which type of external agency includes property developers and TNCs?
A: Corporate bodies
Q: What is one example of a community or local group that influences place meaning?
A: Residents' association (also: civic society, heritage trust)
Q: Why do corporate bodies try to create new place meanings?
A: A better image attracts customers, tenants and profit
Q: How do new place meanings shape people's behaviour?
A: They change where people choose to visit, live, work or invest
Q: Why do some residents oppose a rebranding of their place?
A: It ignores or erases the place meaning they value
Q: What is a formal representation of place?
A: An official, usually statistical representation (e.g. census data, maps)
Q: What is an informal representation of place?
A: A personal, creative representation (e.g. a song, painting, film, blog)
Q: Why do tourist brochures give a one-sided image of a place?
A: They are made to attract visitors, so they show only positive features
Q: Why do informal representations often contrast with census data?
A: They show feelings and lived experience, while statistics show measurable facts
Q: Why is a painting of a place subjective?
A: It mixes the real and the imagined and reflects the artist's view
Q: What is commodification of place?
A: Turning a place's culture or heritage into something to sell, e.g. to tourists
Q: Why does a former industrial area keep an industrial place meaning after the industry closes?
A: Old buildings, street names and memories keep the past visible
Q: How do past processes of development influence present place meaning?
A: They leave a built environment and identity that new uses build on
Q: Why do older residents often call a regenerated dockland 'the docks'?
A: Their memories of the working dock shape their place meaning
Q: What is nostalgia in the context of place?
A: A longing for a place as it was in the past, often idealised
Q: What is NIMBYism?
A: Opposing a development near your home while accepting it elsewhere
Q: Why do residents resist change forced on a place from outside?
A: They have strong attachment and feel they have no control over the change
Q: What is a stakeholder in place change?
A: A person or group with an interest in how a place changes
Q: Why do social media posts give a biased picture of a place?
A: People share selected, often extreme, experiences

## AQA_GEOG_3.2.2.3 | content
Q: What is quantitative data?
A: Numerical data that is measured or counted
Q: What is qualitative data?
A: Non-numerical data such as words, images and opinions
Q: What is geospatial data?
A: Data linked to a location, e.g. by grid reference or latitude and longitude
Q: What is geolocated (geotagged) data?
A: Data such as photos or tweets tagged with the location where they were made
Q: What does GIS stand for?
A: Geographic information system
Q: What does a GIS do?
A: Stores, layers and displays geospatial data on digital maps
Q: Why is GIS useful for investigating place characteristics?
A: Layering datasets shows spatial patterns and links, e.g. deprivation and land use
Q: Which map type shades areas by value, e.g. percentage unemployed per ward?
A: Choropleth map
Q: What is one weakness of a choropleth map?
A: It assumes the value is the same across each whole area
Q: What is a census?
A: An official count of a whole population, asking about people and households
Q: What is one strength of census data for a place study?
A: It covers everyone, so it is reliable and comparable between places
Q: What is one weakness of census data for a place study?
A: It is a snapshot taken only every ten years, so it dates quickly
Q: Why does census data not show lived experience?
A: It records measurable facts, not feelings about a place
Q: What does the Index of Multiple Deprivation measure?
A: Relative deprivation of small areas, combining several domains
Q: Why does an IMD rank not show how much more deprived one area is than another?
A: It measures relative deprivation, not absolute amounts
Q: Why do IMD figures not prove every resident of an area is deprived?
A: They describe the area as a whole, not individuals
Q: What is an oral history?
A: A recorded spoken account of someone's memories of a place
Q: What is one strength of an oral history as a source on a place?
A: It gives first-hand lived experience of the past
Q: What is one weakness of an oral history as a source on a place?
A: Memories fade and are often coloured by nostalgia
Q: What is one strength of a photograph as a source on a place?
A: It shows the built environment at a moment in time
Q: What is one weakness of a photograph as a source on a place?
A: The photographer chooses the view, so it is selective
Q: What is rephotography?
A: Taking a new photo from the same spot as an old one to show change
Q: Why are a historic map and a modern map useful together?
A: Comparing them shows changes in land use and growth over time
Q: What is a semantic differential survey?
A: Rating a place on a scale between opposite words, e.g. ugly to attractive
Q: What is a Likert scale?
A: A scale where respondents rate how much they agree with a statement
Q: What is an environmental quality survey?
A: A survey scoring features such as litter, noise and building condition
Q: Why is an environmental quality survey subjective?
A: Scores depend on the surveyor's judgement
Q: What is content analysis of media?
A: Counting how often words or themes appear in texts or images
Q: What is coding in qualitative analysis?
A: Tagging parts of text or speech with themes to compare them
Q: What is a word cloud?
A: A graphic where more frequent words appear larger
Q: What is sentiment analysis?
A: Classifying text (e.g. tweets) as positive, negative or neutral
Q: Why should a source's intended audience be considered when using it to study a place?
A: The audience shapes what is shown, causing bias
Q: Why do geographers combine quantitative and qualitative sources in a place study?
A: Statistics show measurable change, while qualitative sources show meaning and experience
Q: Why is house price data useful in a place study?
A: It shows economic status and change over time in an area
Q: What is one weakness of house price data in a place study?
A: It shows mainly economic change, not culture or lived experience
Q: What is a questionnaire?
A: A set of questions asked to many people to collect opinions or facts
Q: What is one weakness of an online questionnaire for a place study?
A: Groups such as older people are less likely to answer, so it is biased
Q: What is an in-depth interview?
A: A long, open conversation that explores a person's views in detail
